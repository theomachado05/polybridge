## Families and tags (`research/polybridge_research/schema.py`)

| Family | Value | Tags | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 hedge | `"hedge"` | material_litigation, class_action_filing, regulatory_investigation, cybersecurity_incident, goodwill_impairment, asset_impairment, investment_impairment | `protective_put` |
| H2 opportunity | `"opportunity"` | restructuring_plan, workforce_reduction, facility_closure, business_line_exit | `cash_secured_put` |

`assign_family(tags)` returns `None` for tags from both families or from neither.

## Event (Python)

`Event(ticker: str, filing_date: date, tags: frozenset[str], source: str = "massive_8k", accession_number: str | None = None)` and `.family`.

## Parity (`research/polybridge_research/parity.py`)

`implied = (C_K + P_K) / S_entry` · `implied_scaled = implied·√(sessions_held / dte_sessions)` · `ratio = |S_exit/S_entry − 1| / implied_scaled`

`Proposal = {id, ticker, family, strategy, shares_held, target_coverage, status, basis, label, market, direction, algo, max_contracts, max_notional, created_at, decided_at, bridge_started_at}`

- `basis = "filing_tags"` (family from the pre-registered tag map; `label`, `market`, `direction` null) or `"market_event"` (always `family="hedge"`, `strategy="protective_put"`, `label="Product hedge — no confirmatory claim"`; no filing tags are invented and no research claim is made).
- **Opportunity proposals (options).** `division: "opportunity"` on the market-event path proposes an options trade on the market: `family="opportunity"`, `strategy="options:<structures>"` (from the family's instruments), `label` says the PM-vs-options gap is an estimate and option fills are simulated; `direction` is optional (opportunity ticks are never oriented). `algo` is required and must be one of the Opportunity division's **options families** (`binary_vs_spread_arb`, `vol_vs_pm_move`, `eightk_opportunity`: an `option*` instrument); hedge families and the PM-leg families (`no_bid_seller`, `poly_kalshi_spread`) → 422. A filing-tags opportunity proposal (H2, `cash_secured_put`) may also carry an options `algo`. Every opportunity proposal stores its risk caps: `max_contracts` (default 10, the most structures open at once) and `max_notional` (default $10,000 of premium / max loss at risk). `max_contracts` also caps the family's `contracts` param in `resolved_params` (`capped` lists it; `coverage_cap` is null). Caps on a hedge proposal → 422.
- `algo` (optional on `POST /proposals`, required on the opportunity path): `{"family", "preset_index"? | "params"?, "source": "ai_fit"|"user"}`, the hedgecore algo the bridge will run once approved. Validated against the library (compiled catalog, else `engine/hedgecore/manifest.json`). **Hedge proposals** take hedge-division families only: an options family or a PM-leg family (`poly_kalshi_spread`, `no_bid_seller`) → 422. **Opportunity proposals**, on the market path or the filing path, take options families only (`binary_vs_spread_arb`, `vol_vs_pm_move`, `eightk_opportunity`); anything else → 422. On either: unknown family, `preset_index` ≥ `preset_count`, unknown or out-of-bounds `params`, or both `preset_index` and `params` → 422. Stored pinned: a preset keeps its index; explicit params are completed with the catalog defaults. The server adds `resolved_params` (the exact params that will run), `coverage_cap` and `capped` (`{param: original}` for each size param the cap lowered, else null); these three are ignored in requests. On a hedge proposal `coverage_cap` = the proposal's `target_coverage` and `capped` lists hedge-size params; on an opportunity proposal `coverage_cap` is null and `capped` lists the `contracts` param lowered to `max_contracts`. `null` = no fit (the legacy Engine runs; an opportunity proposal without an algo never reaches hedgecore).
- **The approved `target_coverage` is a hard cap on the algo path.** Every hedge-size param (`coverage`, `max_cov`) above it is lowered to it, at proposal time and again when the bridge resolves the algo (a coverage-1.0 preset on a 0.5 proposal runs with coverage 0.5). The bridge also clips every sell intent so the broker-filled short never exceeds `floor(target_coverage × shares_held)`; this holds families with no such param (`election_hedge`). A sell clipped to 0 is a `fill` with `status: "held"` and an `Algo.on_reject`; a partly clipped one carries `capped_from`.
- `POST /bridges {"proposal_id", "source": "live"|"replay", "market"?, "gap_per_share", "direction"?, "family"?, "preset_index"? | "params"?, "twin"?: {"source", "id", "token_id"?}, "replay_to_account"?, "replay_file"?}`: 404 unknown proposal, 409 not approved, 409 an opportunity proposal **without** an approved options algo; same proposal + same source is idempotent (200), a different source is 409 (one bridge per proposal). On that idempotent re-POST, a body `family`/`preset_index`/`params` that is not the algo the existing bridge runs (including any algo when it runs the legacy Engine) is 409: the caller is never told it succeeded with an algo that is not running. A market-event proposal always uses its own `market` and `direction` (the body's are ignored; `market` may be omitted); a filing proposal needs `market` in the body.
  - **Twin.** A live bridge reads `p_other_venue` from a twin: the body's `twin` when given (validated below), else the **verified twin map** supplies it (`app.twins.twin_of`, reading `backend/app/data/kalshi_twins.json`; only pairs with `direction: "same"`, a Polymarket YES `token_id` and a complete verification record are served; ambiguous pairs never are). A body `twin` that is invalid is 422, never silently replaced by the map. No twin from either → `p_other_venue` NaN. The summary's `twin` is `{source, id, origin}`: `source` is the twin's venue, `id` is what that venue's book is keyed by (a Kalshi ticker, or a Polymarket YES token id), `origin` is `"request"` or `"twin_map"`; `null` when there is none and on replay bridges (replays read `p_other_venue` only from the recording). Tests run against an empty map unless they opt in (`backend/tests/conftest.py`, marker `real_twins`).
  - **Which engine.** The proposal's approved `algo` runs; a body `family`/`preset_index`/`params` that differs from it is 409 (the approval covers what runs). With no proposal algo, the body's choice runs (same validation, 422). With neither, the legacy `Engine` default spec runs (`gap_per_share` applies only to it). The algo is `hedgecore.Algo(family, params, {"shares_held"})`, always with the engine's default direction.
  - **MarketTick per tick** (NaN = not available, never invented; `eightk_score` 0 = none). Live: the primary venue's book, top 5 per side (Polymarket CLOB `/book` for the YES token, or the Kalshi `/markets/{ticker}/orderbook`, whose NO bids are YES asks at 1 − px); `no_bid = 1 − yes_ask`, `no_ask = 1 − yes_bid`; `p_other_venue` = the `twin`'s YES mid on the other venue (422 if `twin` is on the same venue; a Polymarket twin needs `token_id`), else NaN; `under_px` from the broker's quote source (Massive), `under_bid`/`under_ask` only when that quote has a spread; option fields NaN. Only algo bridges poll the equity quote (the legacy Engine never reads it); one quote per ticker per 15 s is shared by all bridges. A previous-close fallback (`massive_prev_close`) is not a current price: `under_px` stays NaN and the tick's `under_source` says `"massive_prev_close (stale: not used)"`. Replay: the recorded mid (`yes_bid = yes_ask = p`), any other MarketTick field the JSONL row carries, and `under_px` from `app/data/equity_bars/<TICKER>.json` as of the row's original time; else NaN. Bars are recorded for the demo replay's tickers (SPY, IWM, XHB, TLT). The summary's `equity_price` says what the algo can see: `"live_quote"`, `"recorded"`, or `"none"` (a replay with no equity price: the hedge families hold with `fee_unknown` on every tick).
  - **Execution.** Each Order intent (equity only for hedge families) cancels the bridge's resting order first (cancel/replace; if it filled meanwhile, that fill is applied), then goes to the active broker as a market order (limit when the intent has `limit_px`; live orders carry the tick's `under_px` as the reference price, replay orders are priced at today's market and labelled). **Replay recorded price:** when the order broker has no current quote for the ticker (Wi-Fi off, Massive unreachable, no `MASSIVE_API_KEY`; checked once per bridge per 15 s; a `no_price` reject marks the quote unavailable for the next order) and the replayed tick has an `under_px`, a replay order carries that replayed `under_px` as `ref_px` with `ref_source: "recorded"`: the SimBroker fills at it ± its half-spread, the order's and the fill's `price_source` is `"recorded"`, the order `note` is `"replay: recorded price (no current quote), not a live fill"` and the `fill` event's `price_note` starts with `"recorded price:"`. A broker that prices its own fills (Webull) is never given a recorded price. Never on a live bridge (a live order's reference is the tick's live quote, or none). A replay with neither a quote nor a recorded `under_px` is still rejected `no_price`. The path applies to sandboxed replays and to `replay_to_account: true`; in the latter case the persistent sim account holds positions at recorded (historical) cost bases, and once a quote returns it marks them at today's price, so the summary's `account_note` (a string, else null) says that the account's unrealized P&L then mixes two price times. `OrderRequest.ref_source` (optional, `^[a-z][a-z0-9_]*$`) names where `ref_px` came from; default `"supplied"`. Filled → `Algo.on_fill("equity", signed qty, fill_px)`; rejected, cancelled/expired, or a broker error/timeout → `Algo.on_reject("equity")`; open → kept as the resting order. `filled_qty` is cumulative per order: a partial fill on an open order is applied once (the resting order remembers `applied`), and a later lookup or cancel applies only the difference. If the broker cannot be read, or a cancel does not take, the resting order is kept (it may still be working), the new intent is held (`status: "held"`, `Algo.on_reject`) and the cancel is retried before the next order and at bridge end; an order the broker no longer lists is treated as expired. A bridge that ends cancels its resting order. The algo bridge's `hedge` is what the broker filled (`hedge_basis = "broker_fill"`); the legacy bridge keeps `"engine_intent"`.
  - **Bridge vs engine replay (same preset, same recording).** A replay bridge feeds `hedgecore.Algo` the MarketTicks the fit replays use (`ReplaySource.fields_for`: mid-only quotes, the recorded `under_px`, oriented once), so with the same fills it makes the same decision on every tick. Pinned on the default demo recording (Another Fed hike 2026 → TLT, `equity_delta_bridge` #75 capped at 50%, 1,000 shares) in `backend/tests/test_bridge_replay_parity.py`: all 401 bridge decisions equal `Algo` stepped on tick time with every order filled (3 orders: sell 378, sell 65, buy 55; 361 `below_sigma`, 37 `inside_band`). **Time.** Replay ticks are stamped on the wall clock (recorded gaps ÷ speed) and `now_ns` is the wall clock. The volatility/sigma gate counts ticks, not time; Staleness compares delivery time (2 s); Session and the wash-sale guard are off in every catalog preset (grid {0}), so these hedge decisions do not depend on the replay speed. Blocks that read the tick's own time do see compressed time on a replay bridge: an explicitly enabled Session or wash-sale guard, and `eightk_opportunity`'s EventWindow (a `window_h` window spans speed × `window_h` of recorded time). None of the shipped recordings carry option or 8-K fields, so no replay bridge opens an EventWindow today; DailyLossCap families trade prediction contracts and never run on a bridge. When the bridge falls behind the schedule (a slow broker call), `ReplaySource` shifts the schedule instead of catching up, so a replay tick is never held `stale` for the backend's own latency. Reject backoff and Cooldown run on `now` (wall clock) by design, because broker calls are real: a bridge whose broker rejects repeatedly holds longer in recorded time than the engine replay, which runs on tick time. **Order counts differ by the fill model, not the decisions.** `hedgecore.replay`'s `n_orders` counts every send. Its fill model refuses an equity fill that has no live quote at a stale recorded close (outside the regular session, or inside one before the price has changed; `n_rejected`), and the algo re-sends a refused order on the next tick. The SimBroker fills a market order at today's Massive quote (or at the recorded price when offline) whenever it arrives. On the demo recording that is 11 engine-replay orders (2 fills + 9 refused sends; uncapped 17 = 4 + 13) against 3 bridge orders (4 uncapped). Give every replay tick a tradable quote and the engine replay places exactly the bridge's 3. Russia/EU → ITA (`energy_geo_hedge` #54, up on YES): engine replay 101 sends / 4 fills at a 50% cap and 136 / 8 uncapped, against 17 and 31 bridge orders. Replay ticks carry no `under_bid`/`under_ask`, so every hedge intent has no `limit_px` and goes to the broker as a market order. After the first refused send the two hedge paths diverge (the engine replay's hedge waits for a fill in session), so the fit's in-sample score describes the session-respecting fills, not the bridge's.

- **Replay market check.** A configured `POLYBRIDGE_REPLAY_PATH` is used only for the market it records. Each recording has a sidecar `<file>.meta.json` (`{source, id, token_id}`); it matches the bridge's resolved market when the venue is the same and the market id or token id matches. A sidecar naming another market is 422, even if the request names the file. With no sidecar the file is used only when the request names it: `replay_file` (an optional file name only, compared with the configured file's name; no path from the request is ever opened) or a market id or token id equal to the file's stem (with or without `-history`). The guessed `replays/<id>.jsonl` file and the live-to-replay fallback respect sidecars too.
- `GET /bridges/{id}` summary includes `replay_file` and `replay_market` (set for replay bridges and when a live bridge falls back to a replay; else null), `shares_held`, `target_coverage`, `coverage` (= hedge / shares_held), `basis`, `label`, `market`, `engine`, `algo` (`{family, preset_index, params (as run, after the cap), source, coverage_cap, capped}` or null), `resting_order`, `cancels`, `hedge_basis`, `twin` (`{source, id, origin: "request"|"twin_map"}` or null, above), `division` (`"hedge"|"opportunity"`), `recorded_price_fills` (replay orders filled at the recorded `under_px`), `account_note` (non-null only when recorded-price replay fills went into the persistent account, above), and on algo bridges `coverage_cap`, `cap_holds`, `equity_price`, `equity_source`.

- **Opportunity bridges** (an approved opportunity proposal with an options algo): `hedgecore.Algo(family, params, {"option": 0})` on raw ticks (never oriented), with one exception: `eightk_opportunity` confirms an 8-K by the PM **adverse** probability, so its ticks go through `app.pipeline.ticks.orient_for_family` (on an "above K" question YES is bullish, so the ticks are flipped as for an `up_on_yes` hedge; on "below K" YES is already adverse). The fit does the same. The SSE/summary PM-vs-options gap always uses raw YES. Option fields come from `app.ticks.OptionsEnricher`: for a threshold question `app.options.match` can map, `enrich_market` fills `opt_mid` (the YES-equivalent structure's per-share mid), `opt_delta`, `opt_iv`, `opt_implied_prob` from the Massive chain snapshot (refreshed every 60 s, network-free in between) and `eightk_score` for a single-stock underlying (NaN when no 8-K data covers the date, and for indices / ETFs); unmapped questions, no key or no listed contracts leave them NaN. Live bridges enrich every tick; replay bridges keep the option fields the recording carries and use the current snapshot only to price legs (labelled). Each Option intent is one **multi-leg option order**, all legs or none (`Broker.place_combo`; the SimBroker prices each leg at the Massive quote mid ± half the quoted spread via `ref_px`/`ref_half_spread`, per-contract fee; `WebullBroker.place_combo` routes to its simulator, labelled). Structures: `binary_vs_spread_arb` → call spread C(k_lo) − C(k_hi) for "above" (put spread P(k_hi) − P(k_lo) for "below"); `vol_vs_pm_move` → straddle at the listed strike nearest K; `eightk_opportunity` → a sell opens a cash-secured put at k_lo, a buy a put spread. Exits trade the open structure's own legs. **Bridge end:** when an opportunity bridge finishes (replay complete) or its source fails, a structure still open is closed on its own legs as one more simulated multi-leg order (`Algo.on_fill` as for an exit); its `fill` adds `close_reason: "bridge_end"` and `close_label` ("simulated close at bridge end ..."), and a `position` event with `close_reason: "bridge_end"` follows. A close that cannot be priced (no chain / quote) is a `rejected` fill and the structure stays in `option_structure`, never hidden. A cancelled bridge task does not close. Fill → `Algo.on_fill("option", ±qty, net per-share structure price)`; reject / no chain / error → `Algo.on_reject("option")`. **Caps:** an order that opens or adds exposure is clipped so open structures ≤ `max_contracts` and risk ≤ `max_notional` (risk per structure ×100: a bought structure's debit at the ask; a sold spread's width − credit; a cash-secured put's strike − credit; a sold straddle counted at its strike); clipped to 0 → `fill {status: "held"}`, `cap_holds` +1. A structure whose quotes give a risk ≤ 0 (stale or crossed legs) is rejected (`Algo.on_reject`), never treated as unlimited `max_notional` room. SSE `tick` adds `options {pm_mid, opt_implied_prob, gap = pm_mid − opt_implied_prob, opt_mid, opt_iv, opt_delta, eightk_score, label}`; `fill` adds `instrument: "option", structure, legs[{ticker, side, qty, status, fill_px, fee, order_id, price_source, quote_mid, quote_half_spread, mark_source}], combo_id, simulated: true, fill_model, quote {net_mid, half_spread}, price_note?, scope?, routed?, capped_from?, cap?, unit_risk?, close_reason?, close_label?` (`fill_px` = the net per-share structure price, `fee` = the sum of leg fees; legs are OCC tickers, quantities whole contracts, ×100 multiplier); `position {option_position, option_structure, risk_used, max_contracts, max_notional, simulated: true}`. The summary adds `division`, `option_position`, `option_structure`, `risk_used`, `max_contracts`, `max_notional`, `option_data` (`"live_chain"|"recorded"|"none"`), `pm_vs_options`, `options_detail`, `fills_label`.

### Options in the fit (`POST /pipeline/fit`)

- Request may add `division?: "hedge"|"opportunity"` (fit that division; null when it has no eligible family) and `end_date?` (the market's resolution date). The response adds the score fields below (additive; existing keys are unchanged).
- For a threshold question that `app.options.match` maps, `app.pipeline.options_join.join_options` joins options-implied history onto the replay ticks: the structure (listed expiry, strikes k_lo / k_hi) from today's snapshot, its history from Massive hourly (else daily) **bar closes** of the two leg contracts, as of each close's bar end; `opt_mid` = the spread's close value, `opt_implied_prob = opt_mid / (k_hi − k_lo) / DF` when in [0, 1]; `opt_iv` = Black–Scholes implied vol (no dividends, r = `RISK_FREE`) of each leg's bar close against the underlying's own Massive bar close as of the same time and the time to the listed expiry (16:00 New York), solved by bisection inside [0.001, 5.0] and interpolated in strike at the matched K; a leg close is paired with a spot close only when both bars closed within one bar interval (the spot series' median bar spacing) of each other, else NaN (stale option bars are not vol moves); NaN where it does not solve (close outside the no-arbitrage bounds, no underlying bar, expired, needs a vol outside the bounds); `opt_delta` stays NaN (no history); `eightk_score` per tick date (NaN where no data covers it, and for indices / ETFs). No key, offline, unmapped, no contracts → ticks unchanged, with a note.
- `available_requirements` meets `listed_options` when any tick has a finite `opt_implied_prob`; only then are the options families shortlisted and scored. The join runs only when the fit can be an opportunity fit (`division: "opportunity"`, or no division and no shares held, or a default fit whose hedge shortlist is empty); a hedge fit skips it.
- **Honest opportunity scores.** An opportunity replay row with `n_orders == 0` is unscored (it measured nothing; a 0.0 would beat every family that traded at a loss). A family whose own signal is NaN on every tick is not replayed: `vol_vs_pm_move` needs `opt_iv` (implied from bar closes as above) **and** `opt_straddle_mid`, a straddle price history: the family trades a straddle at K, while the replay fills and marks every option at `opt_mid`, which the fit builds from the YES-equivalent spread, so scoring it there would describe a spread traded on straddle signals. Nothing builds `opt_straddle_mid` yet, so `vol_vs_pm_move` is never replayed by the fit and its skip reason says "no straddle price history; only the spread proxy exists"; `eightk_opportunity` needs `eightk_score`, `binary_vs_spread_arb` needs `opt_implied_prob`. When nothing is scored the fit returns the rules pick with `score: null`, which the Build screen never offers as an Opportunity.
- **Opportunity score** = replay `pnl` / max(`max_dd`, $1). hedgecore's replay `pnl` is already **net of fees** (every fill's fee is debited from cash before the book is marked; `ReplayStats::pnl`, pinned in `engine/hedgecore/tests/test_replay.cpp`), so the fit never subtracts `fees` again; `fees` is reported in `stats` for display. An explicit `pnl_net` / `net_pnl` from the engine wins.

### Fit score fields (`POST /pipeline/fit`)

- **Hedge fits rank on `hedge_var_reduction_vs_static`**, never on plain `hedge_var_reduction`, which rewards any static short (a fixed short of a fraction h of the shares scores `1 - (1 - h)^2` with no signal; see the `replay.hpp` header comment). The vs-static score is the variance cut beyond a static hedge of the same average size, i.e. what the prediction-market signal adds: 0 = no better than static, negative = timing hurt. It can be negative or near zero; negatives are kept and ranked.
- Response fields: `score` (the ranking score, unchanged name), `score_basis` (`"hedge_var_reduction_vs_static"`, `"net_pnl_per_drawdown"` for opportunity fits, or `null` when unscored), `score_note` (what `score` measures, in words), and, for scored hedge fits only (`null` for opportunity fits and unscored fits), `score_vs_static` (= `score`), `score_raw` (plain `hedge_var_reduction`; reported, never ranked, not the hedge's edge) and `avg_hedge_ratio`. Each alternative's `stats` carries `hedge_var_reduction`, `hedge_var_reduction_vs_static` and `avg_hedge_ratio` as well.
- A hedge preset that never holds a short (no orders, or `avg_hedge_ratio` 0) is **unscored**: its vs-static score is 0 by construction. A preset whose vs-static score is NaN is not ranked. If no preset has one, the fit returns the rules pick (`scored: false`, `score: null`) with reason `NO_STATIC_BENCHMARK`.
- The score is an in-sample replay number (tuned and scored on the same history, best of many presets), not a forecast. Replay fills in the engine respect the US equity session: an equity order with no live quote is rejected outside the regular session and until the price has changed inside it.

### Direction: one orientation, in one place

- `direction` says which outcome hurts a long holder. `app.pipeline.ticks.orient_to_adverse(ticks, direction)` is the **only** place it is applied: afterwards YES means the adverse outcome (for `up_on_yes`: YES ↔ NO quotes, the YES book mirrored as the NO book, `p_other_venue` and `opt_implied_prob` → 1 − p; NaN stays NaN). It takes a dict of arrays (fit replays) or of floats (one live tick).
- The fit pipeline orients hedge-division ticks before `replay_grid`; bridges orient every tick before `Algo.on_tick` and before the legacy `Engine` (which then sees the adverse mid: `p` for `down_on_yes`, `1 − p` for `up_on_yes` on mid-only ticks, as before). SSE `tick` events keep the raw market.
- The backend never passes `direction` to hedgecore (`Algo`, `replay`, `replay_grid` keep their default `"down_on_yes"`). hedgecore's own flip refuses non-hedge families because their intents name the real YES/NO contract; those families are never oriented by the backend and never run on a bridge. For hedge families the engine flip and `orient_to_adverse` give identical intents (tested with the compiled engine).

## hedgecore (`engine/hedgecore`, C++20 + Python module `hedgecore`)

- `HedgeSpec{ticker, shares_held, target_coverage=0.5, band_shares=10, max_hedge_shares=0 (= shares_held), max_staleness_ns=2_000_000_000, sigma_k=2.0, sigma_alpha=0.05, gap_per_share=0 ($/share per unit of dp), fee_per_share=0.0035, half_spread=0.0, min_benefit_ratio=1.0}`
- `Engine(spec).on_tick(ts_ns, p, now_ns) -> Decision{action: hold|order, reason: invalid|stale|below_sigma|inside_band|rebalance|risk_capped|below_fees, order_qty, target_hedge, current_hedge, latency_ns}`
- An invalid `HedgeSpec` (non-finite fields, negative shares/band/cap/sigma_k/staleness/gap_per_share/fee_per_share/half_spread/min_benefit_ratio, `target_coverage` or `sigma_alpha` outside [0,1]) makes every `on_tick` return Hold with reason `invalid`.
- Fee gate (active when `gap_per_share > 0`; inactive at 0 so old callers are unchanged): after the band check, `cost = |qty|·(fee_per_share+half_spread)`, `benefit = |qty|·gap_per_share·|p − p_at_last_order|` (`p_at_last_order` is 0 until the first Order); holds with `below_fees` when `benefit < cost·min_benefit_ratio`.
- `on_fill(qty)` after the broker confirms. Default sizing: `target = round(c · N · p)`, capped.
- A `HedgeSpec` is built only from an **approved** `Proposal`.
- `on_fill` ignores a non-finite qty and latches the engine invalid (every later tick holds with reason `invalid`). A tick dated after `now_ns`, or older than `max_staleness_ns`, holds with reason `stale`.

### Approval to execution

- The bridge starts at most once per approved proposal id (idempotent, keyed by id): never a second `HedgeSpec` from one proposal.
- `family == "hedge"` proposals reach hedgecore as a short stock hedge (`c·N·p`, or the approved hedge algo). `opportunity` proposals reach hedgecore only with an approved options algo (an opportunity bridge, simulated option fills, capped by the approved `max_contracts` / `max_notional`); without one they are executed as a single simulated options order outside hedgecore.
- The backend runs as a single uvicorn worker until the proposal store is persisted (approve-once is per process).
- The backend installs hedgecore only through the uv dependency group `engine` (`uv sync --group engine`); plain `uv sync` excludes it.
