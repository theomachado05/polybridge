## Lead

**Product principle: evidence gating.** Every signal is labelled **validated** (its market passed a pre-registered out-of-sample test) or **unvalidated estimate**, and the label is enforced: while the stock market is closed, PolyBridge plans staged equity orders only on a validated market unless the holder sets an explicit, acknowledged override, and the prediction-market leg (hedge A) runs only on its own opt-in, always labelled an estimate; in regular hours, a hedge on an unvalidated market runs only after an explicit acknowledgement at approval. Every decision, fill and staged order carries its label. Most markets fail, and the product says so. The gate is code, not a slogan: `backend/app/closed/evidence.py` (`signal_status`), the approval route (409 `EVIDENCE_UNVALIDATED` without `ack_unvalidated`) closed-market mode (409 `EVIDENCE_GATE` without an `act_on_unvalidated` the approval acknowledged) and the options-at-the-open trade (409 `evidence_unvalidated` without `ack_unvalidated`); `docs/design.md` section 6. The findings below are the ones that cleared a test; each comes with the scope it holds in. The tests that failed are in the next two sections, with the same prominence.

1. **At the Monday open, options had repriced by only 0.44 of the prediction market's closure move** (95% CI 0.33 to 0.57; 1 = full catch-up, 0 = none). Fresh data: 1,535 events over 44 weekend and holiday closures reopening 2025-10-01 to 2026-09-28; pre-registered as R3, method committed before any price was fetched (`research/results/open_options/SUMMARY.md`, "Answer"; `research/open_options/METHOD.md`). **Scope:** the slope was pre-specified as the reported plain-language measure, not the pass/fail test. R3's pass/fail test is the residual gap net of option costs, which is **+0.79 pt [-1.21, +2.78]**, so R3's verdict is **NULL**: this is information, not a tradable arbitrage. The source also reads the before-cost gap as more like PM overshoot or noise than slow options: after 09:45 the options do not keep moving toward the PM (-0.59 pt [-2.43, +0.88]), the PM gives back part of its move (-3.48 pt [-5.40, -1.62]), and on the 1,535 resolved events the options were better calibrated (Brier 0.120 against the PM's 0.146).
2. **On the US-recession market, the expected-gap model held out of sample in time** (R2, `research/results/gap_model/SUMMARY.md`; `tests.json`). Each closure is predicted with a rate fitted only on closures that ended before it (walk-forward inside the panel). Recession market: sign right in 97 of 151 (64.2%, p < 0.001), slope +1.28 (permutation p < 0.001). Pooled over both panel markets: 141 of 235 (60.0%, p = 0.003), slope +1.25 (permutation p < 0.001), which met R2's pre-set rule. **Scope:** one market. The election market gives 44 of 84 (52.4%, p = 0.744). On the 10-market replication panel it fails: 441 of 878 (50.2%, p = 0.919), slope -0.23 (permutation p = 0.203), 0 of 10 markets pass. R2 is a pre-registered re-analysis of the already-seen 380-closure panel, and most of the open gap stays unexplained (out-of-sample R-squared +0.050).
3. **Staging the equity hedge for the first tradable moment cut post-open variance** (R1 hedge B at 09:30, `research/results/closed_hedge/SUMMARY.md`): **+11.42% [+5.10, +18.14]** against no hedge and **+6.82% [+0.50, +13.54]** against a static hedge of the same average size, on the 09:30 to 10:00 ET P&L of a long SPY holder, 346 of 380 closures. It met R1's pre-set rule (both CIs above 0). **Scope: fragile.** Under the block bootstrap it is only partial (gain over static [-0.71, +14.60]); dropping the 5 closures that contribute most turns the gain over static to -0.78%. It works by timing, not direction: it is active after an adverse expected gap (69 closures), the first 30 minutes after those closures were more volatile (sd 36.5 bp vs 24.8 bp), and hedge size did not predict the direction (correlation +0.00). It cannot reduce the gap itself. R1 re-uses the already-seen 380-closure panel.
4. **The original 380-closure relation is exploratory, and it did not replicate:** over 380 unselected closures in two markets the PM move during the closure lined up with the next SPY opening gap (+7.52 bp per pp, permutation p = 0.001), but on 10 rule-selected new markets the pooled slope was +0.63 bp per pp (date-permutation p = 0.126) (`research/results/leadlag_closed/SUMMARY.md`, Placebo; `research/results/leadlag_replication/SUMMARY.md`). It is a re-reading of the placebo arm of a pre-registered study whose verdict was "mixed", it is same-window co-movement, and it is not compared with futures (section 1).

**Weekend studies in one paragraph (Systematic Trading track; detail and scorecard in section 9).** Twelve further pre-registered studies asked where the prediction-market signal can be traded while stocks and options are shut. Four things hold up. (a) **The link between a question and an asset is real and replicated:** the linked stock or ETF opens where the odds moved overnight, +4.69 bp per point (S4, t = 4.01) and +6.88 on 93 fresh markets (S5, t = 6.01); 34% of links show it on their own against 5% by chance (S14). (b) **It holds inside the weekend, in a market that is open:** Polymarket's own oil price markets move 0.86 points per point of oil-linked event odds while oil futures are shut (S9, t = 5.85), and the event questions move first by minutes (S10). (c) **It is priced by the time it can be traded:** nothing follows after the open (-0.55 bp per point, t = -0.74), under four definitions of the signal and for links picked with hindsight (S14); Friday straddles on flagged tickers do no better than on ordinary weekends, -17.7% of the premium against -17.6% (S7). (d) **Polymarket gives back about 3 points of a large move, and that is venue noise:** 2.63 to 3.20 points in four samples (S8, S9, S15), the cost of crossing the spread; resting orders lose to adverse selection out-of-sample (S12); and on questions that trade on both venues each venue gives back only the moves measured on itself (S16). **No strategy passes its pre-registered criterion.** Every one is reported with its equity curve, Sharpe, drawdown, worst month, turnover, costs, capacity, in-sample against out-of-sample, and every variant tried.

## What didn't work

- **8-K parity, in-sample: NULL for both hypotheses** (H1 hedge, H2 opportunity). No confidence interval excludes zero at any headline horizon (section 3; `research/results/in_sample/hedge_verdict.txt`, `opportunity_verdict.txt`). Out of sample: H1 INSUFFICIENT, H2 NULL (table above).
- **Market-hours lead-lag: no PM lead.** Of 28 usable stress events, 20 had both series move: PM first 9, equity first 9, simultaneous 2; sign test p = 1.000; the pooled tests lean toward equities moving first (section 2; `research/results/leadlag/SUMMARY.md`).
- **Options-versus-PM arbitrage: 5 verified gaps, 0 executable.** Each rests on a single print of 5 to 100 shares. Nothing was traded (section 4; `research/results/arb/SUMMARY.md`).
- **AI fit scores are in-sample only.** Each is tuned and scored on the same history and is the best of many presets. Median 0.0053; 36 of 122 at or below zero; 14 above 0.1. The walk-forward test shows they do not hold out of sample (section 6; `backend/app/data/fits.json`).
- **S1 twin spread: not a pass.** 15 of 99 out-of-sample entries are print-verified, against the 50% the criterion required. The modelled backtest's Sharpe of 7.16 is an artifact of an assumed Polymarket spread (section 9; `research/results/s1_twin_spread/SUMMARY.md`).
- **S4 linked-asset trade: too few observations, and negative.** 12 out-of-sample trades, -47.1 bp net each; the open (and the 08:00 pre-market price) already carries the odds move (section 9; `research/results/s4_linked_assets/SUMMARY.md`).
- **S5 continuation trade: not a pass.** After an overnight odds move of 10 points or more, buying the linked equity at the open lost 14.0 bp per trade on 94 trades [-50.9, +24.1] (section 9; `research/results/s5_big_moves/SUMMARY.md`).
- **S6 Monday fade: too few observations.** 16 out-of-sample trades; 21 print-verified entries in the year, +$14.02 each with an interval through zero (section 9; `research/results/s6_monday_fade/SUMMARY.md`).
- **S7 weekend straddles: a loss.** -17.7% of the premium per flagged trade, no better than ordinary weekends (section 9; `research/results/s7_weekend_straddle/SUMMARY.md`).
- **S8 open referee: null.** Selling an overnight odds move the linked asset did not confirm loses 1.47 points per trade after costs [-2.42, -0.54]; the asset's vote adds nothing (section 9; `research/results/s8_open_referee/SUMMARY.md`).
- **S9 weekend price markets: not a pass.** Selling a weekend move of 5 points or more in Polymarket's oil, gold and stock price markets nets -0.01 points per trade after costs and -3.77 out-of-sample (section 9; `research/results/s9_weekend_price_markets/SUMMARY.md`).

- **S12 resting orders: not a pass.** Resting orders cut the cost by two thirds, but out-of-sample the orders that fill lose 11.02 points more than the ones that do not [-20.79, -5.16] (section 9; `research/results/s12_resting_orders/SUMMARY.md`).

## Product implications

- **Closed-market mode defaults to staged equity orders (hedge B):** an equity hedge prepared while the market is shut and sent at the first tradable moment. It is the only closed-market hedge that met its pre-set rule, labelled with its scope: a known panel, fragile, it changes post-open risk and does not reduce the gap.
- **The PM-contract hedge (hedge A) is opt-in and labelled an unvalidated estimate.** R1 found no evidence it reduces gap variance, and on the replication panel it increased variance.
- **Options at the open is an unvalidated estimate, never a claim.** R3 is NULL after costs, so there is no "opportunity at the open" claim. Its simulated debit-spread trade can be staged only with an explicit acknowledgement (`ack_unvalidated`), needs approval, and passes the option participation caps and the capital budget before it fills.
- **The expected gap is a direction-and-size hint with a wide band**, informative only where a market's own rate is well determined (the recession market passed; the replication markets did not); show the band and the closure count.
- **The AI fit is configuration, not edge.** It chooses and tunes a compiled preset for a market; out of sample the picked preset did not beat a static hedge of the same size.
- **The evidence label is enforced, and every order is sized to the market and the account.** Unvalidated markets need an explicit acknowledgement (and, in closed hours, an explicit override the approval acknowledged); every order path (bridges, staged plans, hedge A's PM leg, the options-at-the-open trade and the manual `POST /orders`) passes participation caps, and every one but hedge A's simulated PM leg passes a capital budget (section 8).

## 1. Closed-market study (source of the 380-closure relation)

- **Question (as pre-registered).** When news breaks while the US equity session is closed (nights, weekends, holidays), does the PM move from the last close to the next open predict the SPY opening gap?
- **Method, one line.** Sign test and slope of SPY gap on oriented PM change over each closure, plus a 380-closure placebo panel and a pairing placebo; rules fixed in `research/leadlag_closed/METHOD.md` before any event-window price was fetched.
- **Sample.** 17 pre-registered news closures, 17 usable (13 use the recession market, 4 the Trump-wins market); 380 placebo closures, 380 usable (`research/results/leadlag_closed/SUMMARY.md`). Event types: weekend 11, overnight 5, holiday 1. Placebo types: overnight 305, weekend 63, holiday 12. The panel is every closure whose start date falls in two fixed date ranges: the Trump-wins market from 2024-04-01 to 2024-11-04 and the recession market from 2025-01-10 to 2025-12-30; each market keeps its pre-set sign and the 17 news closures are removed (`METHOD.md` section 5). Median absolute SPY gap 28 bp on the panel, 94 bp on the news closures.
- **Result on the pre-registered question.** Pre-set verdict: **mixed** (sign test not met; slope met; pairing placebo not met).
  - Same sign in 11 of 15 news closures with a PM move of at least 1 pp (73%, exact p = 0.118); at a 2 pp threshold 9 of 10 (90%, p = 0.021).
  - Slope +10.43 bp of gap per pp (HC3 t = +0.84, R-squared 0.381, permutation p = 0.005); Spearman rho +0.73.
  - Pairing placebo: 7.8 agreeing events on average versus 11 observed (p = 0.075).
  - News closures show no extra response: interaction +2.91 bp per pp (HC3 t = +0.23).
  - Timing test (T4): the PM move up to 08:00 ET does not predict the SPY move from 08:00 ET to the open (7 of 16 agree, slope +0.62 bp per pp, permutation p = 0.278).
- **Result on the placebo panel (re-read, exploratory).** Slope +7.52 bp per pp (HC3 t = +2.58, R-squared 0.042, permutation p = 0.001); 89 of 149 agree at 1 pp (60%, p = 0.021); at 0.5 pp 122 of 207 (59%, p = 0.012); at 2 pp 37 of 59 (63%, p = 0.067); Spearman rho +0.128, p = 0.012 (`tests.json` keys `p1_t1`, `p1_t2`, `p1_by_panel`). By panel at 1 pp: recession 58 of 83 (70%, p < 0.001), election 31 of 66 (47%, p = 0.712). The interaction model's baseline over all 397 closures is the same +7.52 (t = +2.58). **It did not replicate** on 10 new markets (+0.63, p = 0.126; table above).
- **Supports (exploratory).** Over ordinary closures of these two markets, the PM move and the next SPY opening gap moved together, on unselected nights. On the study's own pre-registered question (are news closures special?), the answer is mixed and leans no.
- **Does not support.** A lead, a head start or a tradeable signal: the windows coincide and the timing test is null. Any claim that the PM beats futures or pre-market SPY (305 of 380 closures are overnight, when futures trade; futures were not observed). Any claim that news nights carry more signal. Any claim about the election market. Calling the relation pre-registered, confirmed or replicated.
- **Caveats.** Making the placebo arm a headline was our reading after seeing the results. Events chosen in hindsight (large gaps by construction); events and panel share two markets; 17 events is little power; equities are not literally closed (SPY trades pre/post-market, futures nearly 24 hours); the market-to-news mapping is loose; a 1 pp move means different things at 3% and at 50%; the panel still contains scheduled releases and unflagged news ("no selected news", not "no news"); closures within a market are not independent. See Caveats in the source.

## 2. Lead-lag during market hours

- **Question.** In stress events, does the PM move before the equity does?
- **Method, one line.** First significant move per series (k = 4 rolling sigma, 3-minute change, 5-minute persistence), lead in minutes, minute-level cross-correlation, pooled Granger and HAC regressions with event fixed effects; parameters fixed in `research/leadlag/METHOD.md` before data was fetched.
- **Sample.** 32 candidate events, 28 usable, 4 dropped; 20 events with a significant move in both series (`research/results/leadlag/SUMMARY.md`). 15 of the 28 are scheduled FOMC statements (the unselected part); 13 are curated from memory.
- **Result.**
  - PM first 9, simultaneous 2, equity first 9. Median lead -1.0 min, mean +3.0 min (positive = PM first). Exact sign test 9 of 18, p = 1.000.
  - Same direction in 14 of 20 events (8 of 9 PM-first events).
  - Pooled Granger (10 lags, event fixed effects): PM to equity F = 0.71 (p = 0.716); equity to PM F = 7.72 (p = 2.07e-12). Pre-set verdict: "points the other way".
  - HAC-robust: 0 of 4 Granger Wald tests significant at 5%. Regression A (PM to equity) cumulative response -0.083 (t = -1.21); Regression B (equity to PM) +0.203 (t = +2.29).
  - Sensitivity of the first-move rule: at k = 3, 27 events with both moves, PM first 7, equity first 18, median lead -15.0 min; at k = 5, 16 events, PM first 3, equity first 11. Widening the simultaneous band to 2 min gives PM first 9, simultaneous 5, equity first 6.
- **Supports.** A negative statement: no statistic in this study supports a PM lead in market hours. If anything the pooled tests lean toward equities leading the PM, weakly and not uniformly. Consistent with the PM's use being the closed hours; it does not confirm that.
- **Does not support.** "Prediction markets move first." Also not proof that they never do: individual events exist where the PM was first (9 of 20), for example FOMC 2025-07-30 at +54 min, but 5 of those 9 have the first PM move before the event anchor, so they cannot be reactions to the event.
- **Caveats.** Events are not independent (the 2025 recession market appears in four tariff windows). PM history is at most one point per minute and stale in quiet minutes; the PM series is about 45 to 55 seconds staler than equity at each grid point, which biases leads against the PM (3 of the 9 equity-first events are at -2 min). Thin PM markets print isolated ticks that can pass the rule and bias toward "PM first". Equity moves are measured against SPY/ETF prices only; futures and options are not observed. Curated events were chosen from memory. The per-event HAC columns are oversized and not used. All in the Caveats section of `research/results/leadlag/SUMMARY.md`. **Record disclosure:** the run log's earlier smoke runs and the first full run were deleted from `research/results/leadlag/RUN_LOG.md`; that first full run is reconstructed there from memory and marked as such. The results are the same (28 usable of 32).

## 5. Engine latency and library size

- **Question.** How big is the compiled library, and how long does a decision take?
- **Method, one line.** `hedgecore_bench`: 1,000,000 synthetic ticks per family, default preset, single thread, `-O3`; plus `replay_grid` over 20,000 ticks (`engine/hedgecore/BENCH.md`).
- **Sample / machine.** Apple M5, Apple clang 17.0.0, 2026-10-03, synthetic deterministic tape (LCG seed 42), not recorded market data.
- **Result.**
  - Library: **17 families, 1,386 presets, 37 blocks** (`engine/hedgecore/manifest.json`: `total` 1386, `n_families` 17; `BENCH.md`: "With all 17 families the library has 1,386 presets"). The 17th family, `closed_session_hedge`, was added after the first benchmark run. `docs/library.md` still says 16 families and 1,278 presets; it predates that family. The Library screen and landing page read the count live from the catalog, so they show 1,386.
  - `on_tick` batch mean 27.1 to 34.6 ns across the first 16 families, including its own latency stamp (two clock reads, about 15 ns); `closed_session_hedge` measured 29.7 ns in a second run, inside that range. ## 6. AI fit replay scores (IN-SAMPLE replay, not a forecast; fails out of sample)

- **Question.** Can the pipeline classify a PM market, pick a family and preset from the library, and score it on the market's own history, and how much of that score is the PM signal rather than just hedge size?
- **Method, one line.** `scripts/precompute_fits.py`: classify (keyword rules), shortlist families, build ticks from each market's real price history aligned to Massive bars, `replay_grid`, pick the preset with the best score. Hedge families are ranked on `hedge_var_reduction_vs_static`; `docs/library.md` sections "How the AI picks and tunes" and "What the fit score means".
- **What the numbers are.** Raw hedge variance reduction (`hedge_var_reduction`, shown as `score_raw`) rewards **any** static short of the stock: a fixed short of a fraction h of the shares scores 1 - (1 - h)^2 even when the PM series is constant (`engine/hedgecore/include/hedgecore/replay.hpp`). Ranking on it picks the biggest hedge, so it is reported but never ranked. The ranking score (`score` = `score_vs_static`) is 1 - var(hedged) / var(unhedged x (1 - h)), where h is the average hedge ratio (`avg_hedge_ratio`): the variance cut **beyond a static hedge of the same average size**, i.e. what the PM signal adds. 0 means no better than a static hedge and negative means the timing hurt. A preset that never holds a hedge (no orders, or h = 0) scores exactly 0 by construction, so it is unscored instead (`backend/app/pipeline/tune.py`). These replays also respect the equity session: with no live quote an equity fill is rejected outside the US regular session and until the price has changed inside the session, so a stale close is never booked as hedge P&L (`replay.hpp`, `engine/hedgecore/src/replay.cpp`).
- **Sample.** 133 markets (120 Polymarket, 13 Kalshi; our count of the keys **(tally)**), run 2026-10-03T11:22:21 to 11:22:50+00:00, 29 s (`backend/data_logs/precompute_fits.log` header), provider `rules` (no Gemini), engine library, ticks source `live_history` for all 133, 1,000 shares held; 122 scored, 11 have no fit because the question was classified unsupported (`backend/app/data/fits.json`, `backend/data_logs/precompute_fits.log`).
- **Result.** 122 fitted. Families chosen: `equity_delta_bridge` 62, `crypto_reg_hedge` 22, `tech_reg_hedge` 18, `macro_fed_hedge` 8, `election_hedge` 5, `stress_lead_hedge` 4, `fig_stress` 2, `energy_geo_hedge` 1; 11 unsupported (`fits.json` `summary.families`). Event classes: crypto 47, geopolitics_energy 24, macro_fed 14, tech_regulation 12, company_specific 12, elections 11, unsupported 11, corporate_8k 1, tariffs_trade 1. Distribution of the ranking score `score_vs_static` over the 122 scored fits:
  - 86 are above 0 and 36 at or below 0 (`summary.score_vs_static`: `gt0` 86, `le0` 36); all 36 are negative **(tally)**, meaning even the best preset did worse than a static hedge of the same size. No fit fell back to rules for lack of a static benchmark (`no_static_benchmark` is empty).
  - Median 0.0053, min -0.0596, max 0.508 (`summary.score_vs_static`). The max is `polymarket:4713962`, `energy_geo_hedge` on ITA, raw 0.956 at an average hedge ratio of 0.70 **(tally)**. Quartiles -0.0009 / 0.0053 / 0.0534 and mean 0.037 **(tally)**.
  - Only 53 are above 0.01, 31 above 0.05, 14 above 0.1 and 7 above 0.2 **(tally)**.
  - For comparison, the median raw `hedge_var_reduction` of the picked presets is 0.339 and the median average hedge ratio 0.18; 24 of the 122 picks hedge less than 5 percent on average **(tally)**. So most of what a raw variance-reduction score shows is hedge size, not signal.
  - **The demo markets' own walk-forward rows** (`research/results/fit_oos/per_market.csv`): "Another Fed rate hike in 2026?" -> TLT (`polymarket:4620900`, in-sample +0.257, 4th of 122): the preset picked on the first 60% was #60, train +0.272, **test -0.245**. ITA (`polymarket:4713962`, in-sample +0.508): train +0.631, **test -0.418**.
- **Supports.** The pipeline ran end to end over 133 real markets with no timeouts, none falling back to replay and none without history (`fits.json` `summary`); it was run online (`offline=False`). For a given market it selects the preset whose replay cut the variance of the held stock's P&L the most beyond a static hedge of the same size, on that same history. In-sample the signal adds a material amount on roughly 14 to 31 of the 122 markets (the counts above 0.1 and 0.05) and nothing detectable on most; out of sample, the walk-forward test finds no gain over a static hedge. It is configuration, not edge.
- **Does not support.** That the preset will reduce risk going forward, that a PM predicts the stock, or that the family choice is meaningful. Every score is in-sample and the best of many presets, so positive values of a few thousandths are inside selection noise. Not "86 markets show the signal works". The ticker-to-market mapping is an AI estimate, not a measurement.
- **Caveats.** The replay uses hourly or daily equity bars (`backend/app/pipeline/ticks.py`), so the variance measure rests on few equity changes (31 to 721 ticks per market in the log lines **(tally)**; for example `polymarket:5126779` 91, `polymarket:562829` 721). The top of the range is a few markets with a large average hedge and has not been audited; do not quote a headline score. Classification is keyword rules and some picks look loose (for example "Will Anthropic announce bankruptcy by December 31, 2027?" is classed company_specific and mapped to AMZN). Polymarket history is mid-price only, spread and depth unknown and left empty. An earlier fit run ranked on raw variance reduction; it is superseded and its numbers must not be quoted.

## What the evidence supports (conservative)

1. **Evidence gating is the product's principle,** and the record shows why: of the six pre-registered tests run today, the four on new data failed or were NULL, and the two passes (R2 on the recession market, R1 hedge B) are on an already-seen panel with stated scope.
2. **Options repriced by 0.44 of the PM's closure move at the Monday open** (CI 0.33 to 0.57; 1,535 events, 44 closures, fresh data, R3). After costs the residual gap is +0.79 pt [-1.21, +2.78]: R3 is NULL, so this is information, not a tradable arbitrage, and the source reads it as PM overshoot or noise as much as slow options.
3. **The expected-gap model held out of sample in time on the US-recession market** (97 of 151, 64.2%; slope +1.28); pooled 141 of 235 (60.0%, p = 0.003), slope +1.25, permutation p < 0.001. One market: the election market is 52.4% (p = 0.744), and the replication panel fails (50.2% of 878, slope -0.23).
4. **Staging the equity hedge for 09:30 cut post-open variance** (+11.42% [+5.10, +18.14] vs no hedge; +6.82% [+0.50, +13.54] vs a same-size static hedge). Fragile (partial under block bootstrap; -0.78% gain over static after dropping 5 closures), works by timing not direction, does not touch the gap, already-seen panel.
5. **The 380-closure relation is exploratory and did not replicate** (+7.52 bp per pp, permutation p = 0.001 originally; +0.63, p = 0.126 on 10 new markets). Same-window co-movement, not compared with futures, one of two markets carries it (recession 58 of 83; election 31 of 66, p = 0.712), news nights not special (interaction t = +0.23).
6. **No PM lead in market hours** (PM first 9, equity first 9, simultaneous 2; sign test p = 1.000).
7. **Measured:** the compiled library (17 families, 1,386 presets, 37 blocks) decides in 27.1 to 34.6 ns per `on_tick` on the benchmark tape.
8. **Not found:** an executable PM versus options arbitrage (5 verified, 0 executable). The 8-K study is null in-sample; out of sample H1 INSUFFICIENT, H2 NULL. The PM-contract hedge (R1 hedge A) shows no evidence and increased variance on the replication panel. The AI fit scores are in-sample; out of sample the picks do not beat a static hedge, including on both demo markets (-0.245, -0.418).

9. **Cross-venue gaps on the same question exist but are small and rare** (S1): 15 print-verified out-of-sample entries on 5 pairs lock in +$47.50 on $1,177 of capital after every cost (+$32.28 on 9 entries at 2x costs). The pre-registered test is not a pass, and S3 found no trade.

10. **Linked equities do respond to prediction-market odds while they are closed, and the finding replicated on fresh markets.** S4: +4.69 bp of excess opening gap per point of overnight odds move (t = 4.01). S5, 93 markets S4 never used: +6.88 bp per point (t = 6.01), +137 bp after moves of 10 points or more. The response is complete by the open (after the open: -0.55 bp per point, t = -0.74), so it is information, not a trade.

11. **Polymarket gives back part of a large move, and the give-back is the size of the cost of trading it. Four samples agree.** Event markets after an overnight move of 10 points or more: 2.63 points [-3.90, -1.47] (S5 amendment 3 and S8, two sets of markets). Price markets after a weekend move of 5 points or more: 2.94 points [-4.66, -1.14] (S9), and 3.20 points [-5.13, -1.51] after a rise on 871 markets S9 did not use (S15, quoted prices). A round trip across the spread costs 2.6 to 4.6 points. Resting orders cut that cost and then lose to adverse selection out-of-sample (S12). **The likeliest reason is that the give-back is noise in one venue's price:** on questions that trade on both venues, each venue gives back the 10-point moves measured on itself (Kalshi -3.55, Polymarket -4.30) while the other venue's price does not move (S16; about 30 market-sessions each). It is the size of the spread because it is the spread.

12. **The link between an event question and an asset holds inside the weekend, in a market that is open** (S9): Polymarket's oil price markets move 0.86 points per point of oil-linked event odds while oil futures are shut (t = 5.85). It is co-movement over the same weekend, not a lead, and the fade of the weekend move does not pay after costs.

13. **The missing edge is neither a link problem nor a signal-definition problem, on this evidence** (S14): the gap relation shows link by link (34% of links against 5% by chance) and under every definition of the odds move; links chosen with hindsight for a relation after the open do not keep it.

Do not write that the equity lags the odds after the open, or that S4 found a tradable edge. Do not write that the oil-only weekend fade (S9 V2) is an edge: it is an in-sample variant that lost out-of-sample. Do not write that the twin spread has a Sharpe ratio of 7, or that it is a strategy with capacity. Do not write that prediction markets predict the open or lead equities, that the PM beats futures, that news nights are special, that the 380-closure relation is pre-registered, confirmed or replicated, that options at the open are an arbitrage, that the expected gap works beyond the recession market, that hedge B reduces the gap, that the PM-contract hedge protects, that the fit predicts returns or works out of sample, that the 8-K signal works, or that the scan found arbitrage.
