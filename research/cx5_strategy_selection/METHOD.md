# Selecting the project's strongest paper candidate

This is an audit of already exposed results, begun October 4, 2026 ET. It is
**exploratory selection**, not a new independent out-of-sample test. Prior
strategies, thresholds and verdicts are preserved. A selected subset's old OOS
observations are discovery data for the new subset.

The user asks which existing high-Sharpe strategy has the strongest evidence for
a paper and which micro markets to focus on. Compare source-specific Sharpe
definitions, execution evidence, contractual payoff identity, cost resilience,
independent event count, concentration and portfolio accounting. Do not rank
different annualization conventions as if they were the same metric.

Root compares S1, S6, S11, S17, S18 and S19. Three agents independently audit
S1; S6/S18/S19; and S11/S10/Kalshi-S16. S21 is an active study owned by another
researcher and is not treated as a finished result. No existing studies are
edited, no protected forward recorder observations or sealed 8-K data are read.

Read saved result tables and methodology/code. Preserve source hashes and selected
rows in the comparison snapshot. New public metadata requests are limited to
eight AI/IPO contract records and three S&P contract examples. They verify
current rule text only; they cannot establish historical rule versions. They do
not establish executable quotes, fills, settlement cash flows or capacity.

Any micro-market audit reports all its disclosed cuts, at original and doubled
fees, IS/OOS/ALL where available. Its Sharpe reconstruction retains the original
monthly or other convention and is labeled accordingly. Historical prints are
evidence of transactions, not proof that our algorithm could obtain the same
VWAP, size or queue position. Selecting the largest observed bucket is not
independent validation.

Deliver an evidence-ranked recommendation, exact eligible contract definitions,
an honest paper claim, a prospective validation specification and reproducible
calculations. Distinguish a publishable empirical finding from a verified live
or backtested execution strategy. Do not infer profits from passing code tests.
